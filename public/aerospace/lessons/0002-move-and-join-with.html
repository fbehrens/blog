Reshaping with move & join-with

After this lesson you can predict how move and join-with reshape the tree, and build h[1 v[2 3]] in one command.

Two tools, two jobs

  • join-with <dir> creates nesting. It wraps the focused window and its neighbour into a new container.
  • move <dir> relocates the focused window. Nesting disappears on its own when a container is left with one child (flatten normalization).

Lesson 1 said new windows never create containers. So these two commands (plus layout, next lesson) are how every nested layout is built.

join-with: wrap me and my neighbour

Rule Take the focused window and the nearest node in the direction. Put both into a new container with the opposite orientation of their parent, at the neighbour's old slot.[join-with]
  • join-with right / down: you end up first (top or left) in the new container.
  • join-with left / up: you end up last.
  • The neighbour can be a whole container. Then the new container holds you plus that entire subtree, which can rotate things you didn't expect (see quiz).

Source: JoinWithCommand.swift. It's i3's split, redesigned so it survives flatten normalization.[source]

move: three cases

Look at the focused window's parent. Does it have the move's orientation (h for left/right, v for up/down), and is there a sibling in that direction?[source]

SituationResultExample
1. Swap. The sibling is a window.Trade places.h[1 2 3] move right → h[1 3 2]
2. Move in. The sibling is a container.Dive in and land after that container's most recently focused window (the lesson 1 rule again).h[1 2 v[3 4]] move right → h[1 v[3 2 4]] if 3 was last focused
3. Move out. Wrong orientation, or no sibling that way.Climb until an ancestor has the right orientation. Land right beside the container you climbed out of.h[1 v[2 3]] move left → h[1 3 2]
Edge case: the workspace wall If move-out climbs all the way to the root, AeroSpace wraps the entire old tree in a new root with the move's orientation and puts you beside it. So h[1 2 3] move down → v[h[1 2] 3]: 3 goes full-width at the bottom.

That's --boundaries-action create-implicit-container, the default. It explains the classic surprise: h[1 2 3] move right doesn't do nothing. It gives h[v[1 2] 3].[move]

Recall

State the join-with rule, the three move cases, and the wall edge case. Then reveal.

join-with: me + nearest node in that direction → new container, opposite orientation of their parent, at the neighbour's slot. Right/down puts me first, left/up puts me last.
move: (1) sibling window → swap. (2) sibling container → dive in, land after its most recently focused window. (3) otherwise → climb to the first ancestor with the right orientation, land beside the container I left.
Wall: at the root, the whole tree gets wrapped in a new root with the move's orientation.
