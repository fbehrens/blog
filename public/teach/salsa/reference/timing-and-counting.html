The 8-count

Salsa music phrases in 8 beats (two bars of 4). As an On1 lead you step on six of them and pause on two:

Count12345678
Lead footL fwdR in placeL back—R backL in placeR fwd—
On1 means: you break forward (step against your direction of travel) on beat 1, the strongest beat of the phrase. Pauses on 4 and 8 are where styling and weight transfer live — don't rush through them.

Finding the 1 — what to listen for

  • Conga tumbao: the open double-tap (“tu-tum”) lands on 4-and, 4 / 8-and, 8 — the 1 comes right after it. The easiest landmark in most songs. salsacantante
  • Cowbell (campana): open, loud hits on 1 and 3 — when the bell enters (usually the chorus/montuno section), the 1 is handed to you.
  • Piano montuno: a repeating 8-beat loop; the loop restarts on the 1.
  • Bass: often anticipates (lands on “and of 8”), so don't trust it alone.
  • Phrasing: melodies, vocal lines, and section changes almost always begin on a 1. When the singer starts a verse — that's a 1.

Glossary (timing)

  • On1 — breaking forward on beat 1 (LA style)
  • Break step — the direction-changing step (counts 1 and 5 for the lead)
  • Tumbao — the repeating conga pattern; also the bass pattern
  • Montuno — the repeating piano vamp; also the open jam section of a song
  • Clave — the 2-3 or 3-2 five-hit timeline underlying the music
  • Campana — cowbell played by the bongo player in the montuno section
