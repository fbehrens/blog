AeroSpace layouts · lesson 3

layout: tiles, accordion, orientation

After this lesson you can predict what alt-/ and alt-, do to any tree, including when they rotate your whole screen.

Layout belongs to the container

Windows don't have a layout. Every container has two independent properties:

horizontalvertical
tilesh[…] side by sidev[…] stacked
accordionH[…] one visible, others peek left/rightV[…] one visible, others peek top/bottom

layout changes the focused window's parent container. Focus picks the target, just like in lesson 1.[layout]

Accordion: the focused (most recent) window fills the container. The others are stacked behind it, their edges visible as accordion-padding (30 px in your config). alt-hjkl in the accordion's direction cycles through them.[guide]

Your two keys

alt-slash = 'layout tiles horizontal vertical'
alt-comma = 'layout accordion horizontal vertical'
Rule Walk the arguments left to right. Apply the first one that does not already describe the container. tiles/accordion keep the orientation. horizontal/vertical keep the layout.

So each key is a two-step toggle:

Container isalt-/alt-,
h tiles→ v (flip)→ H (to accordion)
v tiles→ h (flip)→ V (to accordion)
H accordion→ h (to tiles)→ V (flip)
V accordion→ v (to tiles)→ H (flip)

Mnemonic: wrong layout → switch layout. Right layout → flip orientation. / means tiles, , means accordion.[source]

The trap: flipping rotates the ancestors

Rule With normalization on (your config), flipping a nested container's orientation also flips every ancestor, alternating up to the root. Nested containers must stay opposite to their parent.

So in h[1 v[2 3]], alt-/ doesn't put 2 and 3 side by side on the right. It gives v[1 h[2 3]]: 1 on top, 2 and 3 below. The whole screen rotates 90°.[changeOrientation]

  • Switching tiles ↔ accordion never touches ancestors. Only orientation flips propagate.
  • Want 2 and 3 side by side on the right? That's h[1 2 3]. Use move, not layout.

When to use accordion

One slot, many windows, shown one at a time. Classic: h[1 V[2 3 4]], editor left, and on the right a browser, terminal and docs you cycle with alt-j/alt-k. Build it with nesting (lesson 2), focus inside the stack, then alt-,.

Recall

State the argument rule, what each key does, and the rotation trap. Then reveal.

Rule: first argument that doesn't describe the focused window's parent wins.
alt-/: accordion → tiles (same orientation); tiles → flip orientation. alt-,: tiles → accordion; accordion → flip.
Trap: an orientation flip also flips all ancestors (alternating), so a nested flip rotates the whole screen. Layout switches don't propagate.
