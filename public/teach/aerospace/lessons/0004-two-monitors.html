AeroSpace layouts · lesson 4

Two monitors

After this lesson you can predict which monitor a workspace appears on, and move focus, windows and whole workspaces between monitors.

The model: one pool, each workspace has a home

  • All monitors share one pool of workspaces. Each monitor shows exactly one of them (its visible workspace). Two monitors can't show the same workspace.[guide]
  • Every workspace, even an invisible or empty one, belongs to a monitor.
  • Monitors are numbered left to right, starting at 1. main is the one set as main display in System Settings → Displays.
Rule: which monitor does a workspace belong to? The first that applies: (1) its forced assignment from the config, (2) the monitor it's visible on now, (3) the monitor it was last shown on, (4) the main monitor.

Source: workspaceMonitor in Sources/AppBundle/tree/Workspace.swift. Step 3 is the one people forget: a workspace remembers where you last saw it.[source]

workspace X: it comes to its home, and focus goes with it

  • X is visible somewhere: nothing moves, only focus jumps to that monitor.
  • X is hidden: X replaces whatever its home monitor shows, and focus jumps there, even if you were working on the other monitor.
  • X is new or empty: its home is main. Unlike i3, AeroSpace doesn't open it on the monitor you're looking at.
Trap: empty workspaces forget their home An empty, hidden workspace is deleted, along with the monitor it remembered. Show empty workspace 5 on the secondary monitor, switch away, then press alt-5: it opens on main. persistent-workspaces (commented out in your config) prevents this.

Three things you can move

You want to…CommandWhat happens
send this window to the other monitor move-node-to-monitor next It joins the workspace visible there, at the root. Going right into an h root, it lands first (on the side it came from). Otherwise it lands last. Focus stays behind unless you add --focus-follows-window.[source]
send this workspace to the other monitor alt-shift-tab
move-workspace-to-monitor --wrap-around next		 The whole workspace moves and stays focused. The monitor it left shows another workspace that belongs there. Fails for force-assigned workspaces.[cmd]
bring workspace X here summon-workspace X X appears on the focused monitor, wherever it was before. X's home is now this monitor (rule step 3).[cmd]

"Another workspace that belongs there": first the one it showed before, then any hidden workspace that belongs to that monitor, and otherwise the first empty numbered workspace (1, 2, …). That's why a stray empty 4 sometimes shows up. (getStubWorkspace, Workspace.swift)

Focus doesn't cross monitors (yet)

Your alt-h/alt-l is plain focus left|right. Its default is --boundaries workspace, so at the monitor edge it just stops.[focus]

  • focus --boundaries all-monitors-outer-frame right continues onto the next monitor.
  • It lands on that monitor's most recently focused window, not on the one geometrically closest. (FocusCommand.swift)
  • move has the same flag: at the edge, the window goes to the other monitor (like move-node-to-monitor, with focus following). But then you lose lesson 2's "wall" trick (h[1 2 3] + move right → h[v[1 2] 3]) at the inner edge.

Pinning workspaces to monitors

[workspace-to-monitor-force-assignment]
    B = 'secondary'                   # browser always on the second screen
    T = ['secondary', 'main']         # first pattern that matches wins
    1 = 'odyssey'                     # case-insensitive regex on the monitor name

Patterns: main, secondary (only with exactly two monitors), a number (left to right, from 1), or a regex on the name from aerospace list-monitors. A forced workspace always appears on its monitor, summon-workspace can't pull it elsewhere, and move-workspace-to-monitor fails on it.[guide]

The guide's suggested style: one or two dedicated workspaces on the secondary monitor (browser, logs, chat), "the rest" on main. With that, rule step 3 rarely surprises you.

Dojo: do it for real

Right now only one monitor is connected (aerospace list-monitors shows the Odyssey G93SD). Connect a second display first. An iPad via Sidecar works too.

aerospace list-monitors                                     # names + order
aerospace list-workspaces --all --format '%{workspace} %{monitor-name} %{workspace-is-visible}'
  1. Homes. Run the list-workspaces line above. Predict the monitor column before you read it.
  2. Remote switch. Focus the secondary monitor (click), then alt-1. Where does 1 appear, and where is focus?
  3. Move a workspace. On workspace 1, press alt-shift-tab. Predict what main shows now, then check. Run the list-workspaces line again: where is 1's home?
  4. Move a window. Open two dojo windows on main. Run aerospace move-node-to-monitor --focus-follows-window next. Predict whether it lands left or right on the secondary monitor.
  5. The forgetting trap. On the secondary monitor, switch to empty workspace 5 (alt-5 lands on main, so use aerospace summon-workspace 5). Switch away, then alt-5.
  6. Edge. Focus the rightmost window on main, then alt-l. Nothing? Now try aerospace focus --boundaries all-monitors-outer-frame right.

Recall

State the four-step home rule, what workspace X does, the three "move" commands, and the two traps. Then reveal.

Home: forced → visible on → last shown on → main.
workspace X: visible → just focus that monitor. Hidden → it appears on its home monitor and focus follows. New → main.
Move: window → move-node-to-monitor (joins the root there, on the side it came from). Workspace → move-workspace-to-monitor (alt-shift-tab). Bring here → summon-workspace X.
Traps: empty workspaces are deleted and forget their home. Plain focus/move stop at the monitor edge unless you add --boundaries all-monitors-outer-frame.
