Sketchbook · Lesson 1 of the course

From S Pen to Claude Code

Draw one thing, send it to your Mac, and ask Claude about it.

≈ 25 minutes · Galaxy S26 Ultra + Mac · Cheat sheet

The win

By the end you'll have run the whole loop once: draw → save → Quick Share → Mac → Claude Code. Everything later (brushes, layers, comic bubbles, handwriting) goes through this same loop, so it pays to learn it first.

Three ideas before you start

1. The pen draws, your fingers move the canvas. Sketchbook officially supports the Samsung S Pen with real pressure sensitivity, and no setup is needed.[1] If you turn on Pen Mode, only the S Pen draws. Touches from your fingers are ignored for drawing, so your palm won't leave marks. Your fingers then handle the canvas: two fingers to move, pinch to zoom, twist to rotate.[2]

2. Three fingers = time travel. Swipe three fingers left to undo and right to redo.[2] On phones you can also double-tap the bottom-left corner to undo and the bottom-right corner to redo.[3]

3. Save and Share are different things. Save keeps a layered TIFF in Sketchbook's Gallery (Pictures›Sketchbook Gallery). Don't touch those files.[4] Share/Export makes a copy (PNG, JPG…) to send somewhere else.[5] For Claude, send a PNG.

Step by step

A · Set up (once)

  1. On the phone, install Sketchbook (publisher: Sketchbook, Inc.) from the Play Store and open it. Tap + to start a new sketch.

  2. Turn on Pen Mode: open the main menu, choose Pen Mode, and switch it on. Leave Enable Multi-touch Gestures on.[1]

    Test it: drag one finger across the canvas. Nothing should be drawn.

  3. On the phone, check Settings›Connected devices›Quick Share›Share with Apple devices is on. It's on by default on the S26, but only after the March 2026 update.[6]

  4. On the Mac: open Control Center›AirDrop and choose Everyone.[7]

    The Mac's screen must be on and awake, or it won't show up on the phone.[6]

  5. Draw: look around and pick a simple object, like a mug, a lamp or a plant. Draw it with the S Pen in about 2 minutes. Don't aim for nice.

    Press lightly, then hard, while you draw. The line gets thinner and thicker: that's pressure sensitivity.

  6. Change the brush: find the Double Puck, the round control. On a phone you may first have to tap the round marking-menu icon.[3] Drag its top bubble left/right for size and up/down for opacity.[8] Add a thick shadow under your object.

  7. Undo / redo: swipe three fingers left to undo 3 strokes, then swipe right to redo them. Keep your fingers slightly apart and swipe on a slight angle.[2]

  8. Move around: pinch to zoom in on a detail and twist two fingers to rotate. Then two-finger double-tap to fit the whole canvas back on screen.[2]

  9. Save: main menu ›Save.[4] Sketchbook doesn't autosave by default, so get into the habit now.

  10. Send to Mac: main menu ›Share/Export›Share, choose PNG, then Quick Share and tap your Mac.[5] Accept on the Mac if it asks.

    The file lands in ~/Downloads.[7]

  11. Ask Claude: in Claude Code, either drag the PNG from Finder into the window, or type the path:[9]

    What object did I draw? Give me one tip to make it look more 3D. ~/Downloads/<name>.png

    In a plain terminal, dragging a file inserts its path. That's fine, because Claude reads the image from the path.

✓ Done when Claude names your object. If it guessed right, your drawing communicates. That's the whole point of a sketch.
