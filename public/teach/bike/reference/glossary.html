Glossary

The words planners, file formats and Garmin menus use — and what they actually mean. Adhered to in every lesson.

The three things people call "a route"

TermWhat it isWhy you care
Track A dense chain of recorded/computed points (often 1000s), following the actual line of the road. This is what you tour with. It cannot be misinterpreted by the device — the line is the line.
Route (GPX sense) A sparse list of via-points that a device must connect itself using its own map. Fragile. Two devices can produce two different rides from the same file. Avoid for touring.
Course (Garmin sense) Garmin's on-device object: a track + turn cues + elevation profile, navigable and re-routable. Whatever you upload becomes a Course before you can follow it on the Edge.

File formats

FormatCarriesUse it when
.gpxTrack points, waypoints, basic metadata. Open standard.Moving a route between different ecosystems (planner → phone → Garmin). The universal currency.
.fitGarmin's binary format: track + typed course points + turn instructions + climbs.Staying inside Garmin. Richest on-device experience; sideloaded over USB.
.tcxOlder Garmin XML; track + course points.Rarely by choice — Garmin Connect accepts it, that's about it.

Garmin Connect imports GPX and TCX; FIT courses are copied directly to the device's /Garmin/NewFiles or /Garmin/Courses folder over USB. (Garmin Support, komoot)

Points on a line

Waypoint — a named point stored independently of the track (a hotel, a bakery, a water tap). Survives in GPX.

Course point — a point anchored to a distance along the course (turn left here, food at km 43, summit at km 61). Garmin's native concept; announced as you approach it.

Cue / turn instruction — the "in 200 m, turn right" prompt. On an Edge these come either from the file's course points or are generated by matching your track onto the device's routable map.

Maps & routing

Routable map — a map that knows how roads connect, so the device can compute new paths. The Edge Explore 2 ships with the Garmin Cycle Map, which is routable — that is why it can reroute you without a phone.

Snap to road — the planner pulling your clicked point onto the nearest known way. Turning it off gives you a straight line ("as-the-crow-flies").

Routing profile — the ruleset weighting surface, waytype and gradient (e.g. Trekking vs Fastbike in BRouter). Same start and end, very different ride.

Rerouting — device-computed path back to the course after you leave it. The Edge Explore 2 distinguishes course recalculation from route recalculation, and notably does not do U-turns. (Owner's manual)

Breadcrumb navigation — following the drawn line with no turn prompts. The fallback when cues are missing; perfectly rideable, just needs more glances at the screen.
