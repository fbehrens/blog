The Edge Explore 2, feature by feature

Three buttons, one touchscreen, and about a dozen things worth knowing before day one of the tour.

The mental model: three buttons and four gestures

Lesson 1 gave you the pipeline. This lesson is the last box in it — the device — on its own terms. Everything on an Explore 2 is reached through three physical buttons plus four swipes, and once those are automatic you stop fighting the interface and start reading the map.

The gestures, from the home screen: swipe down for widgets (status, weather, and — mid-ride — the controls widget), swipe up for glances, swipe left/right to change activity profile on the home screen or to change data screens while riding, and tap during a ride for the timer overlay with battery life and a way back to the home screen.

Two things a wet Baltic morning will teach you the hard way: a rain-covered touchscreen gets erratic, and your palm counts as a finger. That is what hold power → Lock Screen is for. Learn it now, not in Anklam.

The home screen, in one breath

Navigation (browse map, search, saved locations, mark a location) · Courses (your stages) · History · the settings/menu icon (activity profiles, sensors, safety & tracking, system) · a big ride button with arrows for the biking profile.

The five features that carry a tour

1. Courses — the stage you loaded

Courses → pick a stage → Ride. Before you ride it, open it once and look: select the course to see it on the map, select the climbs graph for per-climb detail, select Summary for distance and ascent. Thirty seconds here prevents the "why is this stage 140 km?" discovery at 11:00.

Two settings hide behind the menu icon inside the Courses list, and both matter: Turn Guidance (turn prompts on/off) and Off Course Warnings (alerts you when you stray). Both on, always, for touring.

2. Off-course recovery

When you leave the line, the Edge offers you a choice: Pause Navigation (freeze it until you rejoin — the right answer for a detour you chose, like a café or a supermarket) or Re-route (pick from suggestions). Know this before it happens: the first rerouting option starts automatically after 10 seconds. If you want to choose, choose fast; if you want to keep your planned line, hit Pause Navigation. And, from Lesson 1: it never asks you to U-turn — it computes forward, sometimes on a scenic 4 km loop when 20 m backwards would have done.

3. Search, Back to Start, Mark Location

These three are the in-field toolkit, and they're the difference between "improvising" and "panicking".

  • Navigation → Search finds a point of interest, city, address, intersection, or coordinates. Use Searching near: to move the search area — searching near your destination for a supermarket at 16:00 beats searching near your current position.
  • Back to Start lives in the controls widget (swipe down mid-ride), and asks Along Same Route or Most Direct Route. On tour the honest use is: the train station you left, or the pension you rode out from for an evening loop.
  • Navigation → Mark Location pins where you are. Water tap, campsite, the ferry slip. They land in Saved Locations and survive the ride.

The neighbouring Navigation → Map menu holds the ones you'll actually look at: Orientation (Track Up is right for navigating, North Up for planning), Auto Zoom, Map Theme (High Contrast earns its keep in flat glare and rain) and the Popularity Map overlay, which darkens well-ridden roads.

Note the shape of this: these live per activity profile. You get three profiles — a sane split is Tour (loaded, cautious routing, big data fields), Day ride, and Indoor/other.

5. Data screens — decide what you want to see

Menu icon → Activity Profiles → profile → Data Screens. Add a screen, pick a layout, double-tap any field to change it. For multi-day touring the four fields that earn their place are Distance to Destination, Time of Day, Distance and Battery — arrival planning, not performance. Also here: ClimbPro (under Climbs), which pops up a dedicated screen at the foot of each climb on a course. Berlin → Usedom will barely wake it up; it is a revelation on a hilly tour.

Housekeeping that pays off on day three

  • Battery: rated up to 16 hours, and that is a rating, not a promise — map redraw, backlight and Bluetooth all take their cut. Lower Brightness and Backlight Timeout under System → Display, and carry a power bank. IPX7 means it survives rain, not a swim.
  • Auto Sleep (per profile) sends it to sleep after five minutes of inactivity. Good for lunch stops, mildly annoying at photo stops.
  • Sensors: menu icon → Sensors → Add Sensor. On tour the two worth having are a Varia radar (cars approaching from behind — genuinely useful on Landstraßen) and, if you ride one, an eBike connection.
  • Phone-connected extras: LiveTrack (menu icon → Safety & Tracking) lets someone at home follow the stage; incident detection can message your emergency contacts; courses can be pushed from Garmin Connect mid-tour. All need the phone in your bag with data.
  • Bike alarm — motion alarm on the device plus an alert on your phone. Worth arming while the loaded bike leans outside a bakery.

Do it now: the kitchen-table drill, then the block

Twenty minutes indoors with the device in your hand, then a short ride. Do it in order — steps 1–6 are free, step 7 is where it becomes yours.

  1. Charge it, then System → About and update firmware via Garmin Connect. Ride nothing on old firmware you haven't tested.
  2. Rename your first activity profile to Tour. Set Navigation → Routing: Routing Mode Road Cycling, Popularity Routing on, Course Recalculation on.
  3. Set Navigation → Map: Orientation Track Up, Map Theme High Contrast. Look at the map with the popularity overlay on — find a road near you it thinks is popular, and decide whether you agree.
  4. Build a data screen with Distance to Destination, Time of Day, Distance, Battery.
  5. In Courses, open the menu icon and confirm Turn Guidance and Off Course Warnings are on.
  6. Practise hold power → Lock Screen and unlocking, five times, until it's muscle memory with gloves on.
  7. Ride your Lesson 1 stage for 3 km — then deliberately turn off it. Watch the off-course warning, count the ten seconds, and try Pause Navigation once and Re-route once. Then use Search to find a café near your destination, and Mark Location where you're standing. Stop, save, and check the ride in History.

Step 7 is the whole lesson. Going off course on purpose, near home, once, is worth more than reading this page three times.

Check yourself

No peeking at the text above — retrieval is the point.

From memory: you left the course on purpose to buy food. Which option?

Pause Navigation. It stops the device recalculating a new line while you run your errand, and picks the course back up when you rejoin it. Re-route is for when you actually want a new way onward.

From memory: where do Routing Mode and Popularity Routing live?

Menu icon → Activity Profiles → your profile → Navigation → Routing. Per profile, not global — which is exactly why a dedicated "Tour" profile is worth making.

From memory: what does the timer button have to do with your ride history?

Everything. History is recorded only while the activity timer is running — navigation works without it, but the ride is not saved. Press start when you roll out.
