Lesson 0001 · ~20 minutes · Mission: Berlin → Usedom

From planner to device

The one pipeline every tour runs through — and your first stage riding on the Edge tonight.

The idea: four boxes, three arrows

Every self-guided tour, from a Sunday loop to a transcontinental, is the same pipeline:

Planner → File → Device → Guidance

Almost every beginner's problem is really a problem at one specific arrow. "My Garmin sent me down a farm track" is an arrow 1 problem (planner settings). "I got no turn prompts" is an arrow 2 or 3 problem (file format, or how it reached the device). Learning to say which arrow is the skill this lesson buys you.

Arrow 1 — Planner → File: track, not route

A track is thousands of points describing the exact line. A route (in the GPX sense) is a handful of via-points that the receiving device connects using its own map and its own opinions. Same file name, wildly different rides. For touring, always export a track. See the glossary.

Your planner's routing profile is where the ride is really decided. Brandenburg is full of sandy Wirtschaftswege — a "shortest" or MTB-flavoured profile will happily push you into deep sand with loaded panniers. Planners disagree here on purpose: komoot's surface labels can be optimistic, while cycle.travel is tuned toward quiet, rideable surfaces. Planning the same stage twice in two planners and comparing is a cheap, powerful check.

Arrow 2 — File → Device: GPX or FIT

PathHowTrade-off
GPX via Garmin Connect Import into Connect (web or phone), then send to the Edge. Easy, wireless, universal. Garmin regenerates turn cues from its own map. Connect accepts GPX and TCX only.
FIT via USB Copy the .fit file into Garmin/NewFiles on the mounted device. Keeps the planner's own turn instructions and course points intact. komoot documents this path.

For Berlin → Usedom, GPX-via-Connect is the right default: it is fewer moving parts, and the Explore 2 has a routable map on board, so it can build its own cues.

Arrow 3 — Device → Guidance

The Edge Explore 2 turns whatever you send into a Course, and reroutes you when you drift off it. One quirk worth knowing before it bites you at km 60: it does not do U-turns. Overshoot a turn and it will compute a fresh way forward rather than telling you to spin around (owner's manual). Lesson 4 will drill the recovery habit.

Primary source — read before the next lesson

The Edge Explore 2 Owner's Manual, section Courses (about six pages). It is dry and it is definitive — worth more than any ten YouTube reviews. For the route itself, the non-commercial Wikivoyage description of the Radfernweg Berlin–Usedom (~337 km, Museumsinsel → Peenemünde) is the honest one.

Do it now: your first stage on the device

Goal: not the whole tour — one stage, end to end, so the pipeline is proven before it matters.

  1. Open a planner (komoot or cycle.travel) on a big screen, not the phone. Set the profile to bike touring / trekking, not mountain bike.
  2. Plan Berlin Museumsinsel → Bernau/Biesenthal-ish, roughly the first 40–60 km of the Radfernweg. Don't optimise it — you are testing plumbing, not choosing the final line.
  3. Scan the surface breakdown. Note the unpaved percentage out loud. This number is the single best predictor of how the day actually feels.
  4. Export as GPX track (not "GPX route").
  5. Import to Garmin Connect → Training & Planning → Courses → Import, then Send to Device.
  6. On the Edge: Navigation → Courses, open it, and press Ride. Watch that a purple line and a turn cue appear. That's the win.
  7. Same GPX to your phone as backup — we will do the Android side properly in Lesson 2.

Check yourself

No peeking at the text above — retrieval is the point.

From memory: name the four boxes of the pipeline.

Planner → File → Device → Guidance. When something goes wrong, name the arrow before you name the fix.

From memory: why is a GPX route a bad thing to tour on?

It stores only sparse via-points, so each device re-invents the line between them with its own map and routing rules. What you saw on the screen is not necessarily what you'll ride.
