Lesson 0003 · ~40 minutes · Mission: Berlin → Usedom

cycle.travel, end to end

One tool that plans the line, splits it into days, finds the beds, feeds the Edge — and rides in your pocket.

Why this planner, for this tour

Lesson 1 said the ride is really decided by the routing profile. cycle.travel's whole personality is that decision: it prefers smooth surfaces and quiet roads, and it says so out loud — "cycle.travel aims to choose a balanced route that prefers smooth surfaces, but will sometimes go off-pavement to avoid hills, busy roads or long detours." For sandy Brandenburg, that bias is worth more than any other feature.

The second reason is the one nobody advertises: it is a planner, not a plotter. Its job is to find a good line from A to B, not to let you trace one you already know. You steer it by dragging, not by drawing. Fight this and you'll hate it; accept it and a 340 km tour takes ten minutes to rough out.

Third: the whole core — routing, turn-by-turn, offline maps, worldwide coverage — is free. It's one developer (Richard), funded by a supporter scheme, with maps updated about monthly.

1 · Planning the line

  • Click a start, click an end. Or type place names into From / To. OpenStreetMap rarely has house numbers — type the street, not the number.
  • Drag the route to move it; a numbered via point appears where you dragged. Click a via point → Remove via to undo it.
  • Extend it: on desktop, tick Click map to add more points; on Android, long-press the new end.
  • Surface toggle: Paths & roads ⇄ Paved only, right under the from/to boxes. You can even constrain one segment: click the via point that starts it and change Go any way to paved. That is the precise tool for "quiet all week, but no sand between Zerpenschleuse and Joachimsthal".
  • Round-trip and Suggest a ride exist for day rides from a single point — useful for a rest-day loop from your Usedom hotel.

Read the result the way the map wants to be read: blue = paved, green = unpaved, with a road-type breakdown in the left panel and an elevation graph on demand. That green fraction is the same number Lesson 1 told you to say out loud.

2 · Splitting 340 km into days

This is the feature that makes cycle.travel worth a dedicated lesson, and it hides in an unlikely place: the Save dialog.

  1. Plan the whole Berlin → Usedom line as one route. Don't pre-chop it.
  2. Click Save, name it, and tick Split into multiple days.
  3. In Split by day, set the number of days or an average distance per day, and optionally Short first day / Short last day — exactly right when day 1 starts after a train and the last day ends at a ferry.
  4. You get one saved journey per day, individually exportable and individually loadable on the Edge.

Then hang the logistics on it: Find hotels shows bookable hotels, campsites, hostels and self-catering along the line; the map key also carries water taps, food stores, cafés, bike shops and repair stations. Plan the stage ends where the beds are, not where the round numbers are.

Supporters additionally get PDF downloads at touring or long-distance scale — or a pure cue-sheet, which is the cheapest possible backup navigator: paper doesn't run out of battery in Anklam.

3 · Getting it onto the Edge Explore 2

cycle.travel has a direct integration, so arrow 2 of the pipeline collapses into one click:

Save the route → GPS button → Send to Garmin Connect → authorise once → it lands in Garmin Connect as a course → Connect app pushes it to the Edge over Bluetooth.

Save before you export: an unnamed download shows up on the device as "cycle.travel", and five stages called "cycle.travel" is its own small hell.

If you'd rather move files by hand, More download options gives you:

GPX trackThe line only, no instructions.The universal currency — phones, any GPS, Garmin Connect import.
TCX courseLine and turns.cycle.travel's own recommendation for Garmin Edge and Wahoo.

Two options worth knowing: pick TCX course or GPX route and you can tick Announce turns in advance, which nudges each cue slightly before the junction so the beep arrives while you can still act on it. And elevation data only rides along if you download from the map page with the elevation graph open — not from the journeys page.

Which path for this tour?

Default to Send to Garmin Connect: fewest moving parts, and the Explore 2 rebuilds its own cues from its on-board map anyway. Reach for TCX course only if you find Garmin's regenerated turns disagreeing with the line you planned.

4 · Does the website sync journeys to the Android app?

Yes — through the account, not through a background sync. One login covers both: "you can use the same account on both the app and the website, so the routes you've planned on the web are accessible in the app (and vice versa)." Your journeys live on cycle.travel's server; the app lists them under Routes.

The nuance that matters on tour:

  • Save on the website first. An unsaved route on screen is not a journey and will not appear anywhere else.
  • Log in with the identical method on both — email/password, or Google, or Apple. Different method = different account = empty Routes list, and this is the single most common complaint in the forum.
  • Listing needs data; riding offline needs a download. To have a stage available with no reception: Routes list → long-press the route name (Android) → download. A phone symbol marks downloaded routes.
  • Download the map too, separately: Settings → Offline map, choose your areas. Recent Android builds can pull offline tiles at the same time as the route. Route without map = directions with a blank screen.

So the honest sentence for the tour: plan on the laptop, save, then — while you still have wifi — open the app and download every stage plus the map corridor. Do that in the hotel, not at the roadside.

The app as your second navigator

Turn-by-turn directions, offline maps and international coverage are free, no region unlocks. Battery: download the maps, then fly in airplane mode, and enable Dim between turns (App Settings) — the screen sleeps between junctions and wakes to tell you. The speedometer doubles as pause/stop; the arrow button recentres the map.

Supporter extras that actually matter touring: offline points-of-interest search (cafés, water, bike shops — working without reception), contours and hill-shading, the high-contrast Wayfarer style, and the extra map library. Sketch mode is iPhone-only so far.

Do it now: the tour, roughed out in one sitting

  1. Create a free cycle.travel account. Write down which login method you used — you'll need the same one on the phone.
  2. Plan Berlin Museumsinsel → Peenemünde as one route. Leave the toggle on Paths & roads for now.
  3. Note the unpaved (green) share and the total. Then flip to Paved only and note both numbers again. The difference between the two versions is your real decision about tyres and daily distance.
  4. Save it with Split into multiple days ticked: try 5 days, Short first day on. Look at where the splits land — town, or forest?
  5. Fix the ugly splits by hand: reopen a day, drag the end to the next real town, re-save.
  6. Click Find hotels and sanity-check that each stage end has somewhere to sleep.
  7. Send day 1 to Garmin Connect and on to the Edge. Compare it against the komoot version from Lesson 1 — same corridor, different opinions.
  8. Install the Android app, log in the same way, confirm all five days appear under Routes, then long-press each one to download, and grab the offline map for the whole Berlin–Usedom corridor.
  9. Put the phone in airplane mode and load day 1. If it still gives directions, your redundancy is real.

Check yourself

No peeking at the text above — retrieval is the point.

From memory: where does the multi-day split live?

In the Save dialog — tick "Split into multiple days", then choose a number of days or an average daily distance, with optional short first and last days. You get one saved journey per day.

From memory: two reasons a stage might be missing from the phone app.

Either you logged in with a different method than on the website (Google vs email = two accounts), or the route was never saved on the website. And a third, for offline: it was listed but never downloaded.

From memory: why save a route before downloading it?

The file takes its name from the saved route. Skip it and every stage arrives on the device called "cycle.travel".
